The AI Readiness of SPRO

A look at where the Strategy, Planning & Revenue Operations team stands on AI adoption — and what to do about it.

By the SPRO Enablement Team Updated May 2026

Across SPRO, the gap between curiosity and capability is wider than headline metrics suggest. Some employees are already automating workflows; others are still asking what AI is for. The chart below plots every member of the org on two axes — how willing they are to adopt AI, and how mature their current use is. Where someone lands determines what they need next.

Four markers of movement

"Up and to the right" isn't a vibe — it's behavior. These are the four things people in the Vanguard quadrant do consistently, and the things every plan below is designed to nudge.

01

Solve from first principles

Reach for the underlying mechanic, not the closest precedent. AI is a force multiplier on the quality of the question you ask — pioneering work starts by reframing the problem, not by automating last quarter's workflow.

Moves you up and right
02

Practice daily

A few deliberate minutes every day beats a workshop every quarter. The tools change weekly; the habit compounds. Try one new prompt, one new feature, one new agent — and notice what changed.

Moves you up
03

Build for the org, not just yourself

Ship a prompt, an agent, a workflow, or an optimization that someone else in SPRO uses tomorrow. Personal productivity is a stepping stone; leverage shows up when your work shortens someone else's week.

Moves you right
04

Share what you learn

Office hours, vidcasts, written playbooks, Slack threads with receipts. Knowledge that doesn't spread doesn't move the org. Teaching is the fastest way to sharpen your own thinking, and the only way Vanguard work compounds.

Moves the org up and right

A badging system

Twelve badges across four tracks — one track per marker, three tiers per track. The badges are the operational definition of "up and to the right": observable, earned, and visible across SPRO.

How they're awarded. Tier I is self-attested and lightweight — log it, move on. Tier II requires a peer artifact and manager sign-off. Tier III is reviewed quarterly by the Enablement team — earned, not given. Earning at least one Tier II in three of four tracks places someone in the Vanguard quadrant; a second Tier III makes them a peer mentor by default.

Plans by Quadrant

Each quadrant calls for a different development plan — meeting people where they are, then routing them toward the next set of badges. Click a quadrant in the chart to highlight it, or read through all four below.

What we're measuring

The two axes are composites of the four markers above. Willingness tracks the inputs — daily practice (#2) and the disposition to approach problems from first principles (#1): stated interest, session attendance, frequency of experimentation, willingness to rework rather than retrofit. Maturity tracks the outputs — what gets built (#3) and what gets shared (#4): tools active in the last 30 days, workflows shipped to peers, playbooks authored, office hours hosted, and peer-visible artifacts produced with AI assistance.

Movement is two-dimensional on purpose. Someone who practices daily but never ships moves up but not right. Someone who ships a single tool and stops moves right but not up. The Vanguard does both, continuously — and teaches the next person how.

The badges above are how movement gets measured in practice. A dot's position on the chart is, formally, a function of which badges someone has earned. Earn more, move further.
